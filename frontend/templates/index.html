Crucial Deficiencies in Indian Demographics

NutriScan focuses on high-prevalence nutritional vulnerabilities identified in national health surveys (NFHS-5).

Iron (Fe) & Anemia Risk

57% Women (NFHS-5)

Cereal-based Indian diets contain high phytate-to-iron molar ratios, inhibiting non-heme iron absorption. NutriScan flags intake deficits and highlights pairing with Vitamin C enhancers (amla, guava, lime).

Clinical Biomarker: Serum Ferritin < 15 µg/L (WHO Anemia Threshold)

Vitamin D Hypovitaminosis

70-90% Urban Indians

Skin pigmentation and indoor lifestyle severely restrict cutaneous cholecalciferol synthesis. Essential for calcium homeostasis, bone density, and neuromuscular health.

Clinical Biomarker: Serum 25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL (IOM / Endocrine Society)

Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Common in Vegetarians

Cobalamin is absent in unfortified plant foods. Deficiencies lead to megaloblastic anemia, peripheral neuropathy, and cognitive fatigue. NutriScan assesses dairy/egg frequency and flags need for fortification.

Clinical Biomarker: Serum B12 < 200 pg/mL (NIH ODS / WHO)

Folate (Vitamin B9)

Essential in Pregnancy

Critical for single-carbon transfer, DNA synthesis, and homocysteine remethylation. Prolonged high-heat cooking of green leafy vegetables causes up to 60% folate loss.

Clinical Biomarker: Serum Folate < 3 ng/mL / RBC Folate < 140 ng/mL

Zinc (Zn) & Immune Function

Phytate-Bound

Zinc is an essential catalytic cofactor in over 300 metalloenzymes. Whole wheat and pulse phytic acid binds zinc in the gut lumen, drastically lowering fractional bioavailability.

Clinical Biomarker: Serum Zinc < 70 µg/dL (IZiNCG / WHO)

Protein Quantity & Quality (PDCAAS)

0.83 g/kg Target

Vegetarian cereal staples often lack lysine, while legumes lack methionine. NutriScan assesses your daily protein intake against ICMR-NIN 2020 and suggests complementary amino acid pairings.

Standard Metric: % Daily ICMR-NIN Reference Intake Fulfilled
